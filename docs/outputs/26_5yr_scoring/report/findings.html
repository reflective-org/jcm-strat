Key findings

No configuration yet gets both the polar vortex and the age of air right. Scored over five years, AL7h, AL7 and AL7t tie at the top (combined 0.93–0.94). AL7 still passes the most metrics (16 of 21). It fixes the vortex, but its stratospheric air is now about two-thirds of a year too young.

  1. The two-year scores flattered the corrected runs. All runs except P20b restart from P20b at the end of 2004, and their age of air keeps getting younger for years after the restart. AL7’s entry-age bias against CLaMS goes from −0.55 years in 2006–07 to −0.70 in 2008–09; AL7h from −0.37 to −0.47; LAL7 from −0.84 to −1.04. P20b, which has run since 1990, holds at −0.14 to −0.16. Over 2006–2010, AL7’s combined score is 0.94 (0.82 on 2006–07), while AL7h stays at 0.93.
  2. The vortex is solved by the extratropical temperature correction, and it holds over five years. AL7’s winter jets match ERA5 within 1–4 m/s in both hemispheres; without the correction (P20b) the southern jet at 10 hPa is 15 m/s too weak (57 vs 72 m/s).
  3. The same correction makes the air too young. Entry-age bias over 2006–2010: P20b −0.15 years, AL7h −0.43, AL7 −0.64. The corrected runs have an upward mass flux at 70 hPa 12–25 % above WACCM6 (6.8–7.6 against 6.05 ×109 kg/s), while P20b is 6 % above it.
  4. Half the correction (AL7h) gives about half of each effect. Southern 10 hPa jet 66 m/s (P20b 57, AL7 75, ERA5 72); age bias two-thirds of AL7’s. Over five winters its northern 10 hPa jet is 18.5 m/s (ERA5 25), so the weak northern vortex seen in 2006–07 was not only sampling.
  5. Keeping the correction out of the subtropics (AL7t) does not help the age (bias −0.63, the same as AL7), but over five years its vortex is the closest of all runs to ERA5.
  6. Upwelling at 100 hPa is too weak in every run (0.13–0.18 against 0.40 mm/s), while the 100 hPa mass flux is close. Nudging the tropical upper troposphere (L0, LAL7) raises it but also makes the air younger; LAL7 is the youngest run (bias −0.97) and scores worst (1.21).
