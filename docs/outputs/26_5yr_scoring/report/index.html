Stratospheric transport scorecard

Six configurations of the dry JCM model, scored against CLaMS, WACCM6 and ERA5 on age of air, the residual circulation and the polar vortex, 2006–2010.

Key findings

No configuration yet gets both the polar vortex and the age of air right. Scored over five years, AL7h, AL7 and AL7t tie at the top (combined 0.93–0.94). AL7 still passes the most metrics (16 of 21). It fixes the vortex, but its stratospheric air is now about two-thirds of a year too young.

  1. The two-year scores flattered the corrected runs. All runs except P20b restart from P20b at the end of 2004, and their age of air keeps getting younger for years after the restart. AL7’s entry-age bias against CLaMS goes from −0.55 years in 2006–07 to −0.70 in 2008–09; AL7h from −0.37 to −0.47; LAL7 from −0.84 to −1.04. P20b, which has run since 1990, holds at −0.14 to −0.16. Over 2006–2010, AL7’s combined score is 0.94 (0.82 on 2006–07), while AL7h stays at 0.93.
  2. The vortex is solved by the extratropical temperature correction, and it holds over five years. AL7’s winter jets match ERA5 within 1–4 m/s in both hemispheres; without the correction (P20b) the southern jet at 10 hPa is 15 m/s too weak (57 vs 72 m/s).
  3. The same correction makes the air too young. Entry-age bias over 2006–2010: P20b −0.15 years, AL7h −0.43, AL7 −0.64. The corrected runs have an upward mass flux at 70 hPa 12–25 % above WACCM6 (6.8–7.6 against 6.05 ×109 kg/s), while P20b is 6 % above it.
  4. Half the correction (AL7h) gives about half of each effect. Southern 10 hPa jet 66 m/s (P20b 57, AL7 75, ERA5 72); age bias two-thirds of AL7’s. Over five winters its northern 10 hPa jet is 18.5 m/s (ERA5 25), so the weak northern vortex seen in 2006–07 was not only sampling.
  5. Keeping the correction out of the subtropics (AL7t) does not help the age (bias −0.63, the same as AL7), but over five years its vortex is the closest of all runs to ERA5.
  6. Upwelling at 100 hPa is too weak in every run (0.13–0.18 against 0.40 mm/s), while the 100 hPa mass flux is close. Nudging the tropical upper troposphere (L0, LAL7) raises it but also makes the air younger; LAL7 is the youngest run (bias −0.97) and scores worst (1.21).

The runs

All runs use the same dry model: temperature relaxation to a Jucker-type equilibrium, Hines and Lott-Miller gravity-wave drag, ERA5 nudging of winds and temperature in the troposphere (including the lowest levels), QBO nudging, T63 with 95 levels. They differ in a correction to the equilibrium temperature in the extratropics, which strengthens the polar vortex, and in how high the ERA5 nudging reaches. All runs except P20b restart from P20b at the end of 2004 and run 2005–2010.

Age of air

The entry age counts time since the air was last below 150 hPa, so it measures transport within the stratosphere only. CLaMS (driven by ERA5) and WACCM6 bracket the plausible range; they disagree by about 0.6 years in the extratropics at 55 hPa.

Residual circulation

The transformed-Eulerian-mean residual circulation carries tracers up in the tropics and down at high latitudes. Its tropical upwelling at 100 hPa (the shallow branch) and higher up (the deep branch) set how fast air ages.

Caveats

The two age references disagree. At 55 hPa between 50° and 70°, CLaMS gives an entry age of 4.0 years and WACCM6 3.4 years. The 2-D age error and the tropics-to-midlatitude step are scored against CLaMS; against WACCM6 the runs would look less young in the extratropics.

Restart runs. AL7, AL7h, L0, LAL7 and AL7t ran 3 years of their own circulation on top of P20b’s 15. Earlier tests found 3-year and 20-year ages within about 0.1 years, but the deep stratosphere could drift a little further.

Two years only (2006–2007). Winter jets carry a few m/s of year-to-year variability; jet latitudes are on the model grid, about 1.9° apart.

Reference periods differ. CLaMS is the 2005–2009 mean, WACCM6 entry age 2005–2009, WACCM6 circulation 1996–2014 and ERA5 circulation 1996–2001. ERA5 winds and temperatures are for the same months as the runs.

Next step

Scaling the correction trades vortex against age almost linearly, so its amplitude alone cannot reach both targets. The young air goes with the stronger 70 hPa circulation the correction drives. Getting both needs a change that strengthens the vortex without speeding up the lower-stratospheric branch, or a slower lower branch on top of AL7.

Method and reproducibility

Model: JCM (jax-gcm) dry configuration in the reflective-org/jcm-strat repository, branches phase23-lat-nudging, phase24-al7-taper and phase25-al7-half. Scores: scripts/success_metrics.py; figures: scripts/report_figures.py; this page: scripts/build_scorecard.py. Ages are averaged over 2006–2010 (the restart year 2005 is left out), circulation from every 6-hourly frame. References: CLaMS v3.1 driven by ERA5, WACCM6 REF-D1 (age) and histSST (circulation), ERA5.
