Stratospheric transport scorecard

Eight configurations of the dry JCM model, scored against CLaMS, WACCM6 and ERA5 on age of air, the residual circulation and the polar vortex, 2006–2010, with a separate group on variability and two physical relations.

Key findings

QBO30, AL7h with the QBO nudging starting at 50 hPa instead of 90 hPa, is still the best configuration: 0.88 on age, circulation and vortex (16 of 21 metrics passed), and second on the new variability group D. Group D shows what the means hide: the seasonal cycle is right, but the stratosphere varies too little, because the northern vortex responds only half as strongly as in ERA5 to the waves coming up from the troposphere. No configuration yet gets both the polar vortex and the age of air right.

  1. Variability and relations (Phase 28, group D, kept out of the combined score).
    • The seasonal cycle is right. The vortex breaks down in spring within 10 days of ERA5 in almost every run, and the annual cycle of tropical upwelling at 70 hPa matches ERA5’s.
    • The stratosphere varies too little. The 10 hPa wind at 60° swings 9–13 m/s from day to day in northern winter against ERA5’s 15. The corrected runs have 3–6 major sudden warmings in 2006–2010, against ERA5’s 8. P20b, with its weak vortex, has 12.
    • The waves coming up from the nudged troposphere are right in every run. But the northern polar cap warms only about half as much per unit of wave forcing as in ERA5 (0.47–0.62 against 0.97 K per K m/s), so the vortex is too stiff. In the south the corrected and QBO runs pass.
    • The tropical age responds 1.6–4.4 times too weakly to changes in upwelling. The upwelling varies about as much as in ERA5, but the model’s age varies three times less than CLaMS’s. Part of that may be because the two ages are defined differently.
    • On group D the QBO runs score best: QBO10d 1.04 and QBO30 1.09. LAL7 scores worst (1.64).
  2. Lighter QBO nudging (Phase 27). QBO30 and QBO10d are AL7h with the QBO nudging loosened in two ways: QBO30 starts the nudging window higher, and QBO10d nudges ten times more gently.
    • Both lower the residual-circulation error (group B 0.88 and 0.90, against 1.05 for AL7h).
    • w* at 50 hPa falls from 0.29 to 0.26–0.27 mm/s, closer to WACCM6 (0.20), and now passes. At 100 hPa it rises a little (0.14 to 0.17–0.18) but is still less than half of WACCM6’s 0.40.
    • The upward mass flux at 70 hPa does not drop (6.90 against 6.80 ×109 kg/s).
    • The tropical entry age is slightly younger than AL7h’s (0.94–0.95 against 1.00 years). The overall age error is unchanged (RMS 0.61 against 0.62 years).
  3. QBO30 keeps the vortex; QBO10d weakens the northern one.
    • QBO30’s jets are within 1 m/s of AL7h’s.
    • In QBO10d the northern jet at 10 hPa drops to 16 m/s and moves to 49°N (ERA5 25 m/s at 62°N), so it scores worse overall (0.99).
    • With 10-day nudging the semiannual oscillation near 1 hPa is weaker and drifts out of phase with ERA5. Below 3 hPa QBO10d’s QBO is almost the same as AL7h’s.
  4. QBO30’s cost is the lowest part of the QBO. At 50 hPa the QBO amplitude drops from 10.6 to 7.0 m/s (ERA5 11.3), and the wind there switches direction 2–3 months after ERA5 does.
  5. The two-year scores flattered the corrected runs (Phase 26).
    • All runs except P20b restart from P20b at the end of 2004, and their air keeps getting younger for years after the restart.
    • Entry-age bias against CLaMS, 2006–07 to 2008–09: AL7 −0.55 to −0.70 years, AL7h −0.37 to −0.47, LAL7 −0.84 to −1.04. P20b, which has run since 1990, holds at −0.14 to −0.16.
  6. The extratropical temperature correction fixes the vortex, and it also makes the air too young.
    • AL7’s winter jets match ERA5 within 1–4 m/s. Without the correction (P20b), the southern jet at 10 hPa is 15 m/s too weak.
    • Entry-age bias over 2006–2010: P20b −0.15 years, AL7h −0.43, AL7 −0.64.
    • Half the correction (AL7h) gives about half of each effect. Moving it poleward (AL7t) does not help the age.
  7. Upwelling at 100 hPa is too weak in every run (0.13–0.18 against 0.40 mm/s), while the 100 hPa mass flux is close to WACCM6’s. Nudging the tropical upper troposphere (L0, LAL7) raises it, but it also makes the air younger, and LAL7 scores worst (1.21).

The runs

All 8 runs are the same dry model (T63, 95 levels; relaxation to an equilibrium temperature, gravity-wave drag, QBO nudging, ERA5 nudging of winds and temperature in the troposphere). They differ in three settings:

  • A correction to the equilibrium temperature in the extratropics, from 400 to 1 hPa. It was obtained by repeatedly nudging the equilibrium temperature towards ERA5 temperatures, and its purpose is to strengthen the polar vortex.
  • How high the ERA5 nudging reaches: up to 400 hPa everywhere, or up to 150 hPa in the tropics.
  • The QBO nudging: the tropical (15°S–15°N, fading out by 25°) zonal-mean wind is relaxed towards ERA5’s monthly mean so that the model has the observed QBO. In all runs but QBO30 and QBO10d it acts from 90 to 1 hPa with a 1-day timescale. QBO30 raises the bottom of that window to 50 hPa; QBO10d lengthens the timescale to 10 days.

P20b has run since 1990. The other 7 start from P20b’s state at the end of 2004 and run 2005–2010; the scores leave out the first year after that restart.

How the scores are combined

  • Each metric’s error is divided by its bound, so 1 means “at the edge of acceptable”.
  • Pass: normalised error at most 1. Near: at most 1.5. Fail: beyond 1.5.
  • A group score (A, B or C) is the mean of the normalised errors in that group.
  • The combined score is the mean of the three group scores, so each group counts equally even though C has 10 metrics and A only 4.
  • There are 21 metrics in total (4 + 7 + 10); “passed” counts how many are at or below 1.
  • The bounds are “moderate”: roughly the size of the disagreement between CLaMS and WACCM6.
  • Group D’s 13 metrics are scored the same way and reported as their own group, outside the combined score.
  • All metrics are computed over 2006–2010.

Age of air

The entry age counts time since the air was last below 150 hPa, so it measures transport within the stratosphere only. CLaMS (driven by ERA5) and WACCM6 bracket the plausible range; they disagree by about 0.6 years in the extratropics at 55 hPa.

Residual circulation

The transformed-Eulerian-mean residual circulation carries tracers up in the tropics and down at high latitudes. Its tropical upwelling at 100 hPa (the shallow branch) and higher up (the deep branch) set how fast air ages.

Lighter QBO nudging: AL7h, QBO30 and QBO10d

The QBO nudging pins the tropical wind to ERA5, and with it the wave drag that drives part of the tropical upwelling. QBO30 and QBO10d loosen it in two ways to see whether the lower-stratospheric upwelling then moves towards WACCM6.

Variability and relations

Groups A–C score multi-year means. Group D asks whether the model also varies like the atmosphere: how much the vortex changes from day to day and from winter to winter, how often it breaks down, when it breaks down in spring, and whether two physical links have the observed strength. The links are the response of the polar vortex to the waves coming up from the troposphere, and the response of the tropical age of air to changes in upwelling. ERA5 here is the full stratospheric reanalysis from the Copernicus Climate Data Store, reduced to daily zonal means.

Caveats

The two age references disagree. At 55 hPa between 50° and 70°, CLaMS gives an entry age of 4.0 years and WACCM6 3.4 years. The 2-D age error and the tropics-to-midlatitude step are scored against CLaMS; against WACCM6 the runs would look less young in the extratropics.

Restart runs. AL7, AL7h, L0, LAL7 and AL7t ran 3 years of their own circulation on top of P20b’s 15. Earlier tests found 3-year and 20-year ages within about 0.1 years, but the deep stratosphere could drift a little further.

Two years only (2006–2007). Winter jets carry a few m/s of year-to-year variability; jet latitudes are on the model grid, about 1.9° apart.

Reference periods differ. CLaMS is the 2005–2009 mean, WACCM6 entry age 2005–2009, WACCM6 circulation 1996–2014 and ERA5 circulation 1996–2001. ERA5 winds and temperatures are for the same months as the runs.

Next step

Scaling the correction trades vortex against age almost linearly, so its amplitude alone cannot reach both targets. The young air goes with the stronger 70 hPa circulation the correction drives. Getting both needs a change that strengthens the vortex without speeding up the lower-stratospheric branch, or a slower lower branch on top of AL7.

Method and reproducibility

Model: JCM (jax-gcm) dry configuration in the reflective-org/jcm-strat repository, branches phase23-lat-nudging, phase24-al7-taper and phase25-al7-half. Scores: scripts/success_metrics.py; figures: scripts/report_figures.py; this page: scripts/build_scorecard.py. Ages are averaged over 2006–2010 (the restart year 2005 is left out), circulation from every 6-hourly frame. References: CLaMS v3.1 driven by ERA5, WACCM6 REF-D1 (age) and histSST (circulation), ERA5.
